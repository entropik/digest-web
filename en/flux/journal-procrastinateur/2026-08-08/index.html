Day 127 – The Builder crosses the entire range

Day 127 of the diary: KEREDIT brings together simulation, layout, client flexibility, PDF output and print run quantities, whilst GOF is cautiously exploring the possibility of SaaS.

Quick read

The work started yesterday in the Template Builder is becoming an almost complete workflow. KEREDIT can simulate a product, distinguish between what will be printed and what is used to present it, manage the customisation options available to the customer, import layouts without distorting them, and then go through the entire workflow right up to the PDF v2. The end of the day brings us back to the business side and clears up another ambiguity: for photo and fine art prints, the quantity sold is the actual number of images, not a value derived from a description or the number of pages.

At GOF, another question arises in the middle of the night: how to demonstrate a future multi-tenant SaaS solution without rewriting the code base or confusing proof with a product promise. The scope remains documentary and limited. It does not alter the current workshop priority.

A Builder that finally lets you try things out

The simulation bench and the ‘Test as the customer’ mode give the Builder a direct verification loop. The test is ephemeral: it alters neither the source document nor its history. Specialised surfaces remain locked when they need to be, the geometry of the coverings is simulated, and the variants already saved no longer disappear in the process.

This simulation also makes it necessary to distinguish between two concepts that were previously too closely linked. ‘Printed composition’ shows the document that will go to production; ‘assembled product’ adds the colours, textures and designs needed to understand the item being sold. The canvas, the page rail and the overall view follow the same convention. The commercial design remains outside the PDF.

Allowing flexibility without losing the template

Client permissions are no longer a pile of implicit exceptions. Explicit profiles and local decisions — inherit, allow, disallow — are handled in the same way by both the frontend and the backend. The composition import preserves these freedoms, the layouts and the elements that the user must still be able to modify.

The pricing grid receives two visible adjustments in parallel: it becomes a proper bordered table, then balances its columns and makes it clear whether a cell represents the total including VAT for the batch or the unit price. When this format × quantity matrix is activated, the effects of the two axes are no longer added a second time to the quotation.

The vertical layout replaces the intuitive approach

The largest batch adds almost no visible functionality. It demonstrates that the Workshop Library, the multi-format Builder, the simulation, the disposable client test, a real client project, the catalogue and publication can all remain consistent within the same workflow. The workflow extends right down to the actual PDF v2 queues and workers.

Two PDF artefacts are observed. The tests also prove the absence of any leakage: neither the product appearance nor the Accurio delivery note must appear in the print-ready document. This boundary is more important than simply the green colour of the corporate identity, as it verifies the terms of the KEREDIT → GOF contract.

Print runs are restored to their actual quantities

The evening session finalises the review feedback on print quantities. The products photo_print and fine_art_print are not paginated products. Their minimum, maximum and pitch settings become authoritative in quantity_rule, and the quantity ordered corresponds to the actual total number of photos or artworks.

The migration of old settings remains unchanged in Prisma, but several fixes are required to limit legacy processing, retain the auto-save function when a price is rejected, and ensure that quotes, shopping baskets and orders all use the same total. Two successive PRs complete the batch: the first delivers the contract, the second addresses the discrepancies found during review.

GOF monitors the SaaS without leaving the workshop

The external repository dépôt local gof receives the commit 211f926. It adds a roadmap, a test PRD and six decisions: explicit organisation and site structure, TenantContext, PostgreSQL and storage isolation, an outbound Accurio connector, modularisation of the table, and a usage ledger separate from invoicing.

The approach remains cautious: retaining the forge, proving isolation with two organisations, and not turning a test into a commercial re-design. KEREDIT remains the authority for orders, projects and assets; GOF remains the consumer of a versioned payload. Today’s post may seem massive because it is: a lot is changing, but the overarching aim is primarily to better separate responsibilities.

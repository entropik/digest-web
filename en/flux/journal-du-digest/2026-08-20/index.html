From the Swiss cartridge to the OOBLIK blog archive

The configurable categories provide the Digest with its system of headings and shared subheadings. This framework then organises the archive of 831 WordPress posts without confusing archived text, source and current destination. Any cases of uncertainty remain subject to human judgement.

Requests and discussions

The day began with a simple need: to be able to manage the main categories without having to edit the files manually. The discussion then broadened the scope to their editorial significance, with the search for a name capable of encompassing philosophy, sociology, ideological struggles and climate, then moved on to defining Krisis and adding a description for each category.

The existing public display, consisting of rounded icons, seemed too generic and difficult to scale up. The preference expressed for Swiss precision and architectural cartouches set the direction for the new design scheme.

Decisions taken

  • The taxonomy is managed from a dedicated section within the administration.
  • A category has a name and a description, even before it contains a link.
  • The public site displays all configured categories, including those with zero entries.
  • Navigation uses a grid of numbered rectangular title blocks.
  • Selecting a box highlights it on the left and reveals the description on the right. The layout switches to a vertical layout on mobile devices.
  • The animations remain informative, accessible and optional.

Changes made

  • adding, renaming and deleting categories via the admin panel.
  • Safe migration of links when renaming, and prevention of the deletion of a category that is still in use.
  • Addition and editing of the ‘description’ field.
  • Categories ‘Cultures & Combats’ and ‘Photographie’ added to the catalogue.
  • Replacement of ‘pills’ with a typographic register featuring lines.
  • Focus mode with descriptive card, explicit back button and keyboard control.
  • Précédent · Folio · Suivant pagination standardised into a compact cartouche without rounded corners.
  • Pagination reset to the actual start of the grid, just below the sticky block, after each folio change.
  • Category counters calculated across all their public links, whilst keeping feeds outside the general Tout filter.
  • Display of empty categories and a fallback message when the description is missing.
  • Successive versions: v1.9.0, v1.10.0 and then v1.11.0.

Importing archives from the OOBLIK blog

Version v1.12.0 adds a one-off, reversible import from a WordPress WXR export. The default mode only generates a validation report. Missing, duplicate, private or redirected posts are never published automatically. The selected posts are added to the feed blog-ooblik and the ‘OOBLIK Blog Archives’ category, and therefore remain outside Tout. Their local images are standardised as 16:9 WebP banners without rounded corners, also available in the modal window.

The patch v1.12.1 enables a subsequent run to recognise these same maps by their destination and source, so as to add the FTP images without recreating the links or altering their IDs.

The patch v1.12.2 reintegrates the 280 WXR posts into Tout, without restoring the old social feeds, and explicitly converts the WordPress categories Photo, Photographes, Argentique, Camera Porn and Exposition into the ‘Digest’ category Photographie, then Livre / Book into Design & Création. The photograph takes precedence when a post also belongs to the ‘book’ category. The other WordPress taxonomies remain as tags, and posts with no matching category retain the archive category.

The visual fix v1.12.3 tightens the internal margins of the category boxes and prevents words from being split in the middle. Développement and Photographie thus remain intact, whilst compound titles can still wrap to the next line between two words. The calendar adopts the same geometry without rounded corners: the panel, navigation, days and markers are square. The current date is filled with the accent colour and the selected date has a square outline.

The ‘v1.13.0’ feature adds a second round of data retrieval for the 525 posts without a ‘Source’ block. The analysis identifies 290 unique external destinations and 63 ambiguous groups. 171 posts with no leads whatsoever are compiled into a standalone HTML review page. The example WordPress post is excluded. The choices are stored locally and can be exported as a overrides.json, without implicit publication.

The following editorial decision treats the 171 posts without a destination as standalone publications, rather than as errors. The import now retains the full text, cleaned of all WordPress posts, including the 280 already present, whilst keeping a short summary on the map. The full text can be viewed in the modal. Posts with no destination use their WordPress permalink as a legacy address.

The local review page — with its asymmetrical headline, strict grid layout with lines, warm paper texture, monospace font and coral accent — is retained as a potential visual reference for another project, without being transposed as such into the Digest.

A second local page adopts this style for the 374 posts still pending approval. It displays the detected issues and the reason for the block, offers the option to choose a destination directly or to archive the post under the WordPress permalink, and exports the decisions by merging them with the 171 existing overrides.

The review ultimately confirms that these 374 entries are not errors: multiple URLs no longer matter once the post is retained as a stand-alone editorial item. The 373 genuine posts are therefore archived under their permalink, as are the 7 posts whose external destination already existed in the Digest. Only the WordPress example is ignored, meaning 831 genuine posts out of 832 WXR publications.

A third review focuses on the 551 posts whose viewing address remains their WordPress permalink. A Brave search excluding the OOBLIK domains finds up to five public destinations per post, classifies them without publishing them and stores the responses locally. Calibration using Marion Parent’s post finds the exact portfolio ‘Deforestation & Palm Oil in Indonesia’ at the top of the list. After identity calibration — distinctive title or recognisable official domain — the complete set produces 244 top suggestions with high confidence, 265 with medium confidence, 40 with low confidence and only 2 posts with no candidates. Eight suggestions already present in the Digest are flagged to avoid duplicates. The choices remain an editorial decision which is then exported to overrides.json.

Automatic validation then retains 240 high-confidence decisions. Two pairs of entries converge on the same destination: for each, one entry adopts the retrieved link whilst the other retains its WordPress permalink. In total, 238 external destinations are thus restored without altering the catalogue’s volume: 4,836 links, 4,836 unique identifiers and no duplicate URLs. A second run of the import confirms the idempotence.

The ‘v1.15.1’ correction then re-examines all archives still attached to their permalinks, including links present in the HTML but not retained by the first validation. It restores a further 113 destinations: 8 unique explicit sources and 105 ordinary links whose identity strongly corresponds to the post. The ÉSAD Pyrénées case is thus linked directly to the current Radical Web Design page. Of the 200 remaining archives, 115 do not actually contain any external links, 6 are duplicates already present in the Digest, and only 79 still require a decision. These 79 cases are compiled at source-gap-review.html. No manual checking of the 831 entries is required. The catalogue remains at 4,836 unique URLs and identifiers.

The link taxonomy has also been streamlined on the existing tag pages . Imported or one-off variants — for example Photographes, Livre / Book or Bernard STIEGLER — are now assigned to their canonical themes. The admin panel rejects new, unregistered keywords; the WordPress importer flags them for review; and the public modal no longer generates URLs from free-form text. A catalogue check prevents any regression to a missing tag path.

The visual tweak v1.15.2 finally clarifies the source in all pop-ups. The destination, the ‘Favourites’ action and ‘Visit the site’ share a compact first line in that order. The OOBLIK Blog permalink is no longer a large, competing button: after a second adjustment, ‘Original post’ becomes a discreet secondary link. On mobile, the three main actions retain the same order, stacking neatly without overflowing.

Validation and deployment

The finalisation at v1.15.2 passes the full repository check: 148 tests of the administration service, 70 tests of the extension, verification of 4,836 URLs and unique identifiers, 813 associated Wayback archives, no unchecked dead archives, and a Hugo build with no warnings. The final modal is tested in a real browser on a computer and then at 375 pixels, with no horizontal overflow and the same order of actions.

The day saw 24 commits on main and 23 runs of the production workflow, all of which were successful. The latest workflow Deploy production published fe113a208. The version v1.15.2 was then confirmed in the public footer and in the logged-in admin area, after which the annotated tag was pushed to the commit that was actually deployed.

Open issues

The heritage processing of visuals is ultimately carried out in v1.16.3 using the local FTP copy, without network access. Of the 823 articles ready for publication, 790 have been given a WebP image cropped to 16:9: 642 featured images, 147 first images of local content and the explicit override for article 711. The first six external images and 27 entries with no suitable candidates remain deliberately unillustrated. The 790 files are all referenced just once. Two successive runs are identical and the catalogue’s heritage fields remain unchanged.

  • Gradually draft the editorial descriptions of the categories via the admin panel.
  • Monitor the grid density once the taxonomy exceeds around ten entries and maintain the ‘cartouche’ layout rather than reverting to a carousel of thumbnails.
  • Examine only the 79 entries compiled in import/wordpress/source-gap-review.html. The 115 records for which there are genuinely no traces and the 6 known duplicates do not require a further review.

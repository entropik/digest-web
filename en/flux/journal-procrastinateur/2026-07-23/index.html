Day 120 – Closing doors that have been left open

Day 120 of the log: KEREDIT locks down templates, GOF sharing, authentication, assets, tenant and commerce boundaries, then adds quotas and purges uploads.

Quick read

The night of 22nd to 23rd does not change the subject: it changes the scale. After stabilising the GOF iterations and regenerations, KEREDIT is closing, one by one, the remaining open doors around the product.

This isn’t a day of spectacular new features. It’s a day when the beta learns to say no: to reject a bypassed permission, an unsigned CSRF token, an unaccepted artefact, an overly permissive URL, a tenant leak or an unlimited upload.

Templates regain authority

The first commits finalise the previous day’s work on the template variants. Fine-grained permissions are resolved before the merge between variant and project, legacy flags are assigned explicit values, the exact variant is cleaned up before validation, and frozen shares progress atomically towards the correct rendering.

The server no longer relies solely on the client’s interpretation. The geometry, visual identity and permissions locks become a backend contract, which protects both the client editor and the builder.

The shared content follows the correct GOF iteration

Several PRs ensure the share is consistent with support regenerations. The public PDF follows the GOF job and the iteration actually rendered, accepts the latest web artefact still readable from a failed production run, requires an accepted PDF and retains a viewable version whilst a new one is being generated.

The expected result is simple: a sharing link must neither accidentally display an old file, nor cause a valid PDF to disappear because the subsequent production run encountered an incident.

Authentication and URLs become context-sensitive

The ‘Better Auth’ hardening ties CSRF to the session context, partitions tokens, enforces a twelve-character length for the relevant forms, and rejects unsigned tokens as soon as a session exists. At the same time, asset URLs validate their original target, serve SVGs as attachments, use a dedicated secret and calculate their TTL from the actual time of issue.

These are two versions of the same principle: a proof is only valid within its context, for a limited duration and for a specific purpose.

Workers must tell the truth

Catalogues and preview updates are aligned with the work actually being processed. An asset is no longer marked as ‘pending’ if no job exists, and a future thumbnail key is no longer persisted until the actual completion of processing. The back office therefore stops displaying promises that the worker cannot yet fulfil.

Closing the day by setting limits

The end of the session tightens public boundaries, staff access to tenant-scope, WooCommerce secrets, bearer authentication and the entropy of the GOF pairing code. Above all, it adds upload quotas, session rate limits and the purging of abandoned uploads.

Following this merge, Git shows no further KEREDIT or GOF activity until 30 July. Looking back on 4 August, this lull also reflects the passage of time, holidays and fatigue. The break is not a gap to be glossed over: it forms part of the project’s actual rhythm.

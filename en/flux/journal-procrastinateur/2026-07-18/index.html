Day 116 – The forge finally breaks the one-minute barrier

Day 116 of the log: GOF and KEREDIT have abandoned large-scale reports and concurrent jobs in favour of a simple Production v2, which has been tested and validated on both live production environments without being sent to Accurio.

Brief summary

The day begins with a stark realisation. The 1075844 command ran through WooCommerce and KEREDIT correctly, but both the web PDF and the workshop PDF failed in GOF. Both machines are ready; their CPUs and memory are not saturated, and yet the leases are expiring whilst the 59 resources are being prepared.

The diagnosis pinpoints the real problem: with each step in the process, the control plane rewrites a now-massive PostgreSQL snapshot whilst holding a lock. The agent waits ten seconds, abandons the call, and the scheduler eventually considers its lease to be lost. The sophisticated system no longer protects the rendering; it prevents it.

The solution is therefore very simple: do not tinker with the timeout; redesign the architecture until reliability, stability and speed are achieved on both production environments.

Replace the large state with small truths

GOF Production v2 separates Production, its Families, Attempts, Leases, progressions, artefacts and Accurio Deliveries into normalised tables. A progression no longer depends on the size of the history. Each lease carries a fencing mechanism; an expired agent can no longer publish or complete the work of a subsequent cycle.

The global snapshot, dual-write and a new broker have been ruled out. The choice is not to add yet another layer, but to have a clear line for each persistent fact and an append-only log for transitions.

The cover sheet and the workshop booklet become an atomic family. The web PDF is another, independent family. The acceptance of the PDF and its submission to Accurio are two separate decisions: a compliant file is never sent simply because it has been accepted.

Unify the KEREDIT contract as well

KEREDIT previously produced a print job and a web PDF job that could compete for the same project version. The v2 contract retains a single key per order, project and version. Families are added solely by monotonic union; an identical replay has no effect and a contradiction is rejected.

The BullMQ intent is persisted prior to publication. KEREDIT jobs close together under a lock. Pollers carry a cycle token. A late response can no longer overwrite a terminal state, and a previous failed admission can only be automatically readmitted once, following a GOF fail-closed read.

Cloud paths are retained for future use, but remain at v1. They do not serve as a silent fallback for the P340/i5 path.

Correcting operational noise

A second issue emerged during the final audit. Every 60 seconds, the GOF ‘reconcile’ forces Caddy to reload an identical configuration to refresh the Docker aliases. The QUIC listener is recreated and Alt-Svc errors appear even though the route is functioning correctly.

The fix utilises Caddy’s dynamic DNS upstreams. An identical configuration is only validated. A genuine change is reloaded before being persisted, so that a failed reload can be retried. Five timer cycles then elapse with zero reloads, zero new listeners and zero Alt-Svc errors.

Testing the actual path, without affecting Accurio

The command 1075844 is first retrieved without re-rendering: its artefacts, which are already present, are reconciled and captured. This demonstrates retrieval, not the performance of the new build system.

An existing layout then completes in GOF in approximately 8.3 seconds. Two new magazines each produce a web PDF, a section and a cover on both forges in approximately 32 seconds on the KEREDIT side. All families pass on the first attempt.

The final test, carried out after the GOF deployment 0.4.2, uses a new virtual command. P340 renders the web family in 9.2 seconds and i5 renders the workshop family in 22.6 seconds. The entire process, including Accurio checks and captures, completes in 45.7 seconds.

The PDFs are checked both structurally and visually. The reference zine contains 61 web pages, 60 booklet pages and a cover. No clipping, overlapping, black squares or corrupted assets are observed. The checksums of the accepted artefacts and the captures match.

Accurio remains shut down in two places. The control plane disables live operation and the P340 Gateway remains in capture mode. All submissions end with ‘ validated_not_sent’; no actual transmission takes place.

One hundred per cent, with the correct phrase

Following the final test, the 11 v2 Productions are settled, the 21 Families are accepted and the 21 Accurio Remises are validated_not_sent. No jobs, leases or transfers are currently active. The two forges are ready and the logs from both the control plane and the dashboard contain no errors during the final run.

The final campaign therefore achieved 100 per cent compliant runs on its observed sample. This figure is accurate, but it must not be treated as an absolute statistical guarantee for any future orders. Long-term reliability will come from the repetition of the same consistent practices, not from a slogan.

Ultimately, success hinges on a few key factors: a Production environment, explicit families, leases closed once, small increments, two interchangeable forges, and no implicit transmission.

The system has become more reliable because it has finally agreed to become simpler once again.

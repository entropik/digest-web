Day 134 – The OVH switchover becomes proof of production

Day 134 of the log: KEREDIT and GOF have approved OVH 3-AZ Paris for production, without an Accurio acceptance handover and without load regression testing.

Session timetable

  • Agent used: Codex
  • Primary environment: Mac, KEREDIT production on a Hetzner VPS in Germany, OVH 3-AZ Paris, P340/i5 build servers and GOF repository dépôt local gof
  • Date covered: Sunday 23 August 2026
  • Main branches: feat/gof-admission-test-flux-flag, fix/e2e-journey-test-flux-echo, fix/test-flux-live-recovery-guard (KEREDIT branch); GOF PRs #90 and #91
  • Time window selected for the minimum: 09:59 – 16:33 CEST, KEREDIT and GOF tracks crossed
  • Minimum duration observed: approximately 6h34+
  • Version delivered: 0.22.8, release 9855526e; GOF aligned with 9c324f9
  • Main traces: PR KEREDIT #451 to #454, PR GOF #90/#91, green production acceptance and post-switchover campaign k6
  • Minimum cumulative time since the start of the log: approximately 866h13+ (859h39+ as at 22 August, + 6h34+ today)

Quick summary

The switchover is now effective and verifiable. KEREDIT now writes to krampouz-assets-prod at OVH 3-AZ Paris, GOF reads the assets and writes its artifacts to them, and the forges continue to receive only pre-signed ephemeral URLs. Customer data is stored in France; the core application remains on the European VPS in Germany.

This sovereignty-focused choice goes live with two pieces of evidence: no Accurio handover is triggered by the acceptance test, and performance is not compromised.

Resolving the risk identified the previous day

The ‘test_flux’ marker now passes through KEREDIT and GOF all the way to the persistent production environment. Readmissions where the marker diverges are refused. The KEREDIT acceptance test fails early if it encounters an unmarked live production environment and requires the marker to be echoed upon admission.

The final proof is deliberately robust: Auto Accurio remains active, the acceptance Production is marked test_flux, delivery equals none, transmitted remains false and no Accurio delivery lines are created. The complete PDF workflow is therefore executed on OVH without any risk of delivering a test job to the print shop.

Measure from Europe, don’t just take the provider’s word for it

The raw benchmark is run again from the VPS and from the production workstation. From the VPS, OVH is approximately 3.2 times faster than R2 on the pre-signed PUT of 4 Mio, 4 times faster on the signed GET and 3.4 times faster on the SDK PUT. From the workshop, the performance gains observed remain between 2.3 and 2.5 times.

The k6 campaign confirms the application’s behaviour: 1,693 complete iterations and 127,440 checks completed without failure on the editor-beta scenario at 50 VUs. The variants served by the proxy are 2 to 2.6 times faster, whilst the batch upload remains on a par because it is primarily dominated by the worker.

The log provides a slight caveat: the SQL aggregate failed_count did not match the campaign’s fixed accounts. The zero-failure rate is therefore proven by the k6 and /admin/storage checks, not by this unavailable aggregate. The 429 errors observed under load stem from the IP-based limiter and necessitate ensuring compliance with Retry-After.

What the switch means for OOBLIK

Photos, previews, PDFs and production artefacts now have their primary residence in France with a European provider, whilst the application and database are operated in Germany. This France-Germany split gives OOBLIK a clear EU framework for its customers’ data.

R2 remains accessible for read-only access during the grace period: sovereignty is not used as a pretext for removing the safety net too soon. A complete exit from Cloudflare, a second cross-provider copy and the final purge remain separate decisions to be taken at a later date.

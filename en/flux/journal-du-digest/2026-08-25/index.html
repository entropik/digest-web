Repeat without deleting on LinkedIn

A LinkedIn resource can be republished without deleting its previous posts. The author field remains editable and SQLite retains every instance. The technical log becomes a standalone public mini-blog, without being incorporated into the OOBLIK Blog archives.

Requests and exchanges

  • The ‘published’ status should no longer act as a definitive barrier.
  • Repeated editorial content was to remain distinct from accidental duplicates.
  • Each new post should retain its own URN and date without overwriting the history.
  • The browsing experience had to work without ambiguity across multiple computers.
  • The development blog was to be integrated into Flux as a standalone editorial section, with a chronological index, full-text viewing and RSS.
  • The legacy feed blog-ooblik was to remain strictly unchanged.
  • The index summaries had to be no more than three sentences long.
  • Each map was to feature a visual banner, reproduced as a square image in the full post, based on American posters from the 1940s and 1950s.

Decisions

  • A standard submission remains deduplicated and links to the most recent known post.
  • ‘Repost on LinkedIn’ is an explicit action that reopens the composer before requiring further confirmation.
  • The URL-based lock and the ‘submitting’ status remain unchanged in order to protect against concurrent submissions and ambiguous remote results.
  • docs/journal/YYYY-MM-DD.md files feed directly into the public pages via a Hugo content adapter. No copies of the posts are retained in content/.
  • The visual corpus is sourced from the IIIF feed of Northwestern University Libraries. Only ‘War posters, American’ dated from 1940 to 1959 and labelled ‘No Copyright – United States’ are included. Their source and attribution remain visible.

Implementation

  • Automatic migration from linkedin_publications to a historical append-only index organised by URL and date.
  • Addition of an occurrence counter to publication responses.
  • Reading of the central history upon opening the composer in order to directly activate ‘Confirm republishing’ on all workstations.
  • Replacement of the retrieval process with a repeatable editorial republishing process, with editable text and hashtags.
  • Update of public, technical and operational documentation.
  • Upgraded the site and administration version to v1.18.1 following the integration of the changes made on 25 August.
  • Addition of the ‘Digest Journal’ banner in Flux, an index by year and by month, dedicated pages with folio numbers, and a standalone RSS feed.
  • Strict validation of post titles, dates and summaries, accompanied by a consistency check between the Markdown files, the nine pages generated and the RSS feed.
  • Co-ordinated migration of the website and the administration panel to v1.21.0 for this new public feature.
  • Reproducible creation of a local corpus of one hundred WebP posters, with a metadata catalogue, attribution and an IIIF collection script.
  • Addition of banners on the index page, a square box within blog posts and an automatic check limiting each summary to three sentences.
  • Co-ordinated migration of the website and the administration system to v1.21.1.

The Birth of the Digest and the Restoration of Lost Links

The Digest gives a new form to an older practice: hand-curated selections of links in HTML. It brings together in one place WordPress blogs, Tumblr, Instagram and other scattered traces, without removing URLs that have become unstable or are no longer active.

The project therefore does not begin with a bookmarking service. It builds on hand-written Digests, fed by the OOBLIK blogs and by posts shared over the years on Tumblr, Instagram and other social media platforms. The changes reflect four successive requirements:

  1. to make the old HTML Digests searchable again.
  2. to bring together links from WordPress blogs and social media feeds without erasing their sources.
  3. to have a static, readable Digest, organised and deployable without revealing any server secrets to GitHub.
  4. to make this diverse archive searchable by archives and tags.

The restoration revealed two editorial considerations: missing resources deserve to be preserved and documented, whilst old, empty Mailchimp editions provide no useful historical record.

Decisions

  • Hugo and PaperMod form the public backbone.
  • data/links.json centralises the links. The archives consist of dated Hugo content.
  • The Digest is not a platform migration: it brings together multiple sources, multiple websites and multiple generations of URLs.
  • GitHub Actions creates a ‘production’ branch. CloudPanel takes over without any webhooks or server credentials in GitHub.
  • A dead public URL remains viewable and is flagged as such.
  • Domain variants and the actual validity of the destination must be verified, not just their HTTP response.
  • All external links open in a new tab.

Implementation

The first commit adds the theme, the homepage, the archives, the tags, the catalogue and the curation skill. The validation and deployment workflows follow immediately.

The first historical restoration brings back old editions and several social feeds. A Pinboard export is one of the technical sources processed that day, alongside other batches: it is neither the core of the Digest nor its origin. The import tools, consistency checks and the concept of ‘lien-mort’ instead provide a common method for these fragments. Imported titles are cleaned up, external links corrected and the tag explorer is given a dedicated interface.

Above all, this first day marks the start of a wider project. Posts from WordPress blogs, notably the OOBLIK Blog, will subsequently be added to the same repository along with their text and provenance. Tumblr, Instagram and other feeds will retain their identity rather than being merged into a single source.

Mailchimp entries that no longer contained a usable destination and six entirely empty editions have been removed. This deletion concerns import artefacts, not documented defunct public resources.

Validation and deployment

The added workflows validate the data and then build Hugo. The 16 GitHub Actions runs visible for the day were successful, with no failures. The final deployment goes through the `production` branch and is handled by CloudPanel.

Result

At the end of the day, the hand-crafted HTML digests have found a sustainable long-term solution. The site now has its own editorial model, an initial history, its quality controls and a reproducible production workflow capable of accommodating blogs and scattered feeds.

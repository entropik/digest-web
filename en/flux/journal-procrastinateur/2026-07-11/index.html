Day 110 – From customer support to distributed forges

Day 110 of the log: KEREDIT Projects and Support section, followed by the construction of the GOF Flux pilot system, distributed across two forges isolated from production.

Quick read

The day starts with the client and ends in the workshop. KEREDIT makes the space Projects more peaceful, adds rounded-corner guides to the notebooks and opens a help centre directly within the editor. GOF, for its part, stops treating the second machine as merely a hardware candidate: a distributed, isolated driver begins to redistribute commands between two forges.

The heatwave and fatigue remain the backdrop. They haven’t resulted in an unproductive day, but they reinforce the need for an architecture that is clear, reversible and testable without affecting existing production.

KEREDIT resolves three visible issues

The ‘Mon compte > Projets’ workflow is now treated as a proper user interface. The search, sort and action tools remain visible whilst scrolling; actions are grouped together and technical labels are better contained. Several close-up iterations were required to achieve the right density and spacing without reopening the commerce flow.

Notebooks now display rounded corners within the canvas and in page previews. The guide remains a production-related piece of information: it does not alter the project’s persistent geometry.

The latest release primarily adds a permanent help centre to the editor. It is searchable, tailored to the product family and accessible from several points within the workflow. Help is no longer documentation to be found outside the product; it guides the user’s actions at the very moment they need it.

GOF moves from research to pilot

This morning’s cloud research still involved choosing a runtime and a future ForgeCore. The rest of the day has delivered an initial distributed system, albeit deliberately limited in scope: flux-test.keredit.com hosts the dashboard and the control panel, whilst the P340 and the i5 each run an agent in a separate distributed-test space.

The legacy GOF at /opt/[chemin privé], its database and forge.keredit.com remain outside the scope. This separation is the project’s main safeguard: the pilot may fail, be reset or be stopped without requiring the restoration of current production.

The new ‘Forges’ menu displays presence, heartbeat, CPU, RAM, slots and active work. The operator can set a forge to active, paused or maintenance mode. Distributed jobs retain their PDFs, rendering reports, validation reports, hashes and casting proofs. An expired lease can be taken over by another machine without losing the production contract or mixing up the reports.

Optimising forges, not just a single machine

The performance issue remains the same: a 60-page zine still takes several minutes when pages and transformations follow one another. The pilot does not yet demonstrate that the future PDF engine can process a file in under a minute. It first seeks to prove that a single contract can be accepted, assigned, rendered and checked across multiple nodes without revealing R2 or Accurio secrets to agents.

The approach therefore splits into two complementary tasks: distributing capacity and reducing the cost of rendering. Image pre-compilation, the content-addressed NVMe cache and the Sharp terminal pool remain optimisations to be measured within the renderer. Adding forges must not be used to mask an unnecessarily sequential hot path.

What remains to be proven

The project is ongoing, not yet complete. Acceptance testing must still confirm two active forges using the same font release, the assignment and subsequent resumption of a job following lease loss, the integrity of the artefacts, and the Accurio dry run. The actual Accurio submission remains doubly locked.

The production decision will follow, based on the golden samples and the measured times. For now, the day has yielded something more useful than a promise of speed: a comprehensive, isolated and reversible test environment to optimise the forges without putting the workshop at risk.

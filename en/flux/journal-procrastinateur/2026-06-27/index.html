Day 96 – Templates become a language

A day spent organising the photo layout templates for the notebooks: page navigation, covers, templates, sorting and the sequence for automatic filling.

Overall summary

The previous day had separated two topics that tended to get mixed up too easily: the web PDF on the one hand, the workshop print on the other. Today we return to the editor, but with the same underlying idea: a visual interface is not enough; a contract is needed.

For photo albums, the contract is beginning to take the form of templates. A template is not just a pretty layout. It is a reusable rule that specifies how many photos a page can hold, or where they fall, how a page responds to the chosen number of photos, and how automatic filling can offer something other than a bare grid.

Autofill is therefore evolving into a layout language, not just a magic button.

What has been built

Today’s commits show a fairly clear progression:

  • a1beea2 - simplifying a group of shape layers;
  • 7110d73 - adding canvas/page navigation arrows;
  • ac7c69e - widening the controls at the bottom of the editor;
  • 9e1f4bd - clarifying the page navigation controls;
  • 49331f7 - clarifying the spine and cover guides in books;
  • 3dfdd38 - add photo layout templates for books;
  • d91b738 - add sorting and spacing to auto-fill;
  • e057667 - add ‘full photo’ templates;
  • 42cbaee - make photo layout settings live;
  • 488eb52 - save photo layout templates.

The key point is the shift from static to interactive: layouts are no longer simply coded as arbitrary positions; they can now be selected, varied and saved.

Covers, double-page spreads and limits

The book project also continues to highlight that a book is not a homogeneous sequence of ordinary pages.

The front cover, inside front cover, inside back cover, spine and the pages of the inner section do not all serve the same purpose. Navigation must therefore remain clear: you can view a double-page spread, but certain areas are not editable and certain zones belong to the cover as a whole rather than to the inner section.

The guidelines for the spine and cover have been clarified to avoid a simple but dangerous misunderstanding: believing that what is visible on screen necessarily corresponds to a customer-editable page.

Autofill: the first true logic

Autofill now has two functions:

  • photo sorting;
  • page layout.

This is still a work in progress, not a final UX. But the principle is established: the client should not have to compose each page by hand to achieve a coherent album, nor should the tool impose a monotonous grid.

The “full-photo” templates add a much-needed sense of breathing space. A photo book needs dense pages, but also pages that allow a single image to stand on its own.

What this day reveals

The day highlights a clear product direction:

photos chargees -> tri -> rythme -> gabarits -> proposition de carnet

There is still a lot of UX work to be done. But the technical foundation is starting to speak the right language. Auto-fill must not be a black box; it must be a suggestion that the user can understand, modify, and dismiss without losing their flow.

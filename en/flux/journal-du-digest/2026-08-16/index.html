Publication, visual identity and sharing on LinkedIn

The administration gains a post in a single action, with trackable progress. The site is given its full identity and a native LinkedIn share feature reserved for the owner. Thirty-three commits bring the catalogue to 3,992 links and 836 edits.

Requests and correspondence

The requests recorded in the plans and documentation concerned:

  • simplifying the publication process and providing immediate feedback on its actual progress.
  • automatic resumption of tracking after an interruption.
  • a more visible OOBLIK identity in the browser and in social media previews.
  • a large LinkedIn image, followed by a native post where the text remains editable.
  • a customised configuration of LinkedIn credentials without exposing secrets to the browser.
  • The option to share either an entire edition or a specific link.
  • Correction of editorial destinations without losing their history.

The remainder of this summary is reconstructed from the commits and their diffs. The full conversations are not stored in the repository.

Decisions

  • Progress tracking uses four server states — preparation, validation, deployment and release — and no fake percentages.
  • A single timer tracks the publication process and resumes after a page refresh, a return to the tab or a temporary network error.
  • The production workflow builds Hugo once and deploys that same output.
  • The site version is visible in the footer and follows SemVer.
  • Social media images are deterministic, versioned and limited to an optimised 1200 × 627 PNG.
  • Native LinkedIn sharing is an admin action. Secrets and tokens are encrypted in SQLite and are never sent back to the browser.
  • The suggested text, including hashtags, remains editable before submission.
  • Correcting or reactivating a link preserves its identity and editorial history.

Development

Publication and operation

The redundant preview button has been removed in favour of a single action. A four-segment progress bar displays the persistent states, resumes automatically and leads to public publication. Line breaks are standardised to ensure consistency when switching between Windows and WSL.

The Hugo build avoids repeated searches in the catalogue. Production validates the sources and the extension, builds once, then publishes this artefact to the production branch. The extension’s archive is also verified in CI. The footer now displays the site version.

Brand identity and social media images

OOBLIK favicons are added for the site, Safari, iOS and the admin panel. A deterministic graphic system generates a social poster for each archive, with a grid, typography, flat colours and variants derived from the content. The images are included in the social metadata and visible on the editing pages. Long lists of posters are paginated.

Catalogue editing

A link’s URL can now be edited via the admin panel. Several corrected destinations, including Éditions GwinZegal, are reactivated following verification of their identity. A YouTube entry is corrected without deleting its history.

LinkedIn publication

The author now has access to an enlarged, refreshable preview, and can then post a large image accompanied by text and a link. The mechanism is now a native integration with the LinkedIn API via OAuth, configurable via /admin, with encryption of credentials and the token, customisable text and automatic addition of hashtags. The action remains invisible outside the admin panel and now finally supports the sharing of an individual link.

The ‘About’ page also documents the technical and editorial background to the Digest.

Validation and deployment

  • Initial testing of the publication tracking feature comprised 39 successful admin tests and 70 successful extension tests, using TypeScript compilation and a Hugo build.
  • Subsequent developments added contract tests for social media images, LinkedIn configuration, OAuth, native publishing and administration actions.
  • The majority of the work was merged into main via pull requests nos. 17 to 43. The detailed status of the workflows can still be viewed in GitHub Actions.
  • The catalogue checked at the end of the day contains 3,992 links and 836 edits.

Day 112 – Moving from the pilot phase to a fully operational system

Day 112 of the diary: GOF Flux becomes canonical, the old monolith is rolled back, and KEREDIT enhances support, security, deployment and cost estimates.

Quick read

The previous day had demonstrated that two forges could render, revert to their previous state and send a valid command to Accurio. 13 July raises the following question: what needs to be shut down for this proof to become a workable system?

GOF Flux takes the canonical address of the forge and the old monolith is removed from the nominal path, without destroying its rollback. KEREDIT, for its part, transforms several functions that are still isolated into operational contracts: manageable assistance, better-scoped authentication, valid uploads, observable readiness, transactional deployment and prices revalidated before payment.

This is not a day of new product promises. It is a day when existing promises begin to support real-world operation.

GOF Flux becomes the canonical forge

forge.keredit.com , moving away from the legacy VPS and pointing towards the distributed control plane. flux-test.keredit.com remains an alias, but KEREDIT retains its URL and tokens: the switch does not require reconfiguring the client contract.

The final visible functional change concerns split ledgers. The historical request print-master is now resolved to cover_pdf and cahier_pdf prior to the creation of the distributed job. A KEREDIT token concludes with its cover, its ledger and six control artefacts.

The removal remains reversible. The monolith’s queues are checked to ensure they are empty; a 4.2 Gio archive preserves PostgreSQL, Redis, configuration, fonts, goldens and staging, after which the historical containers are stopped without deleting their volumes. The rollback follows a simple procedure: repoint the DNS, recreate the historical stack and verify its status.

The P340 and i5 remain ‘ready’. The Accurio live locks remain in place. The new system is canonical, but this does not mean that a successful acceptance test grants permanent authorisation to deploy to production.

Help becomes manageable content

The customer help centre launched yesterday is no longer limited to content embedded in the frontend. ‘/admin/help’ now allows you to manage categories, chapters, the homepage, tooltips and their visibility by product family.

The Tiptap editor supports headings, lists, quotes, links and sidebars. A screenshot library remains separate from customer photos. Publication is atomic, and the built-in fallback continues to function if the API is not available.

This mini-CMS remains deliberately limited in scope. KEREDIT is not becoming a general-purpose content platform; it simply gains the functionality needed to explain its own operations without having to redeploy the application every time text is corrected.

Closing the boundaries between client and tenant

Today’s work has tightened up several entry points that could still lead to ambiguous states. Password credentials are synchronised, Better Auth is tenant-scoped, and upload payloads are validated before processing.

Client projects benefit from more precisely scoped deletions, the public PDF is streamed rather than loaded entirely into memory, and the editor launch wait timer can be reset properly. Taken individually, these changes are modest. Taken together, they remove bottlenecks that often only become apparent when dealing with real accounts and real volumes.

Deployment becomes a transaction

Readiness explicitly checks PostgreSQL, Redis and storage. Redis probes have a real-time timeout: a blocked service should no longer be mistaken for a service that is simply slow.

The VPS deployment sequence carries out backups, migrations, business snapshots, smoke checks and provenance verification before updating the release markers. Checks fail quickly, and the last validated release remains the definitive version if an attempt does not complete.

The ‘test-flux’ guard is aligned with GOF statuses, export timestamps and historical logs. The pipeline no longer simply seeks to run all its scripts: it must be able to explain at which point it refuses to continue.

The price is revalidated before payment

The e-commerce system finally addresses a significant vulnerability. Each shopping basket quote is linked to the persisted version of the project and to a SHA-256 hash recalculated by KEREDIT. Any changes to a price, quantity, format or option must no longer persist unnoticed until payment.

The legacy pricing structure photo_print is clarified as a unit price per copy. The OOBLIK live replay is documented; tests cover the consistency of the quote and the WordPress 0.1.82 connector is prepared with the same revalidation contract.

Support procedures close the loop with eight incident categories: login, projects, assets, pricing, orders, PDFs, Printbox and alerts. The beta doesn’t just need code that works; it needs a clear path to follow when it doesn’t.

What remains open

The promotion of GOF Flux does not bring the HA project to a close, nor does it complete the qualification of future remote forges. The historical archive remains a rollback, not a second production environment to be left running in parallel.

As for KEREDIT, the quote is now finalised, but the photo_print workflow still needs to be fully integrated with WooCommerce. The following day will build on this foundation: enabling photo prints to be ordered, then taking the reliability of backups and runtime much further.

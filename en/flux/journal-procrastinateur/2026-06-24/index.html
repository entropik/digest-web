Catch-up post for 24 June – The blog is finding its rhythm

Post from 24 June: WooCommerce clarifies the BAT and web PDF options; images finally retain their orientation and dimensions; and auto-fill transforms the notebook into a proper layout.

  • Main environment: VS Code WSL, Codex plugin, KEREDIT repository
  • Date covered: Wednesday 24 June 2026
  • Branch observed: dev

  • Minimum duration observed: approximately 6 hours 2 minutes, for 20 commits
  • Version reached in the sequence: KEREDIT 0.9.22
  • Main batches: WooCommerce shopping basket options, EXIF orientation, photo insertion, cover area, automatic notebook filling
  • Minimum cumulative time since the start of the log: approximately 555h13+ (548h47+ published as at 23 June, plus 0h24+ from 1 May reintegrated, plus 6h02+ today)

A quick read

This day had fallen between two posts. Yet it deserves its own entry: it elevates several still-fragile actions from the status of local fixes to that of established behaviour.

The WooCommerce basket is becoming clearer, adding a photo is no longer a surprise, and the automatic filling of the album is starting to offer a rhythm rather than simply piling up images. It’s a day of rapid development, with a downside that the next session will reveal: many UI features were developed simultaneously, before being sufficiently validated by testing.

The shopping basket finally tells you what it’s selling

The morning begins with a series of minor fixes to the . The BAT and web PDF options are moved back under the project, displayed as radio buttons, then forced and restored after the initial adjustments. Customer labels are synchronised, the options message is clarified and the product total is better framed.

Taken individually, each commit seems minuscule. Together, they address a key commercial issue: at the point of ordering, the customer must understand what they are adding to the project and not encounter a different interface between KEREDIT and the WordPress shopping basket.

A photo must appear exactly as it was taken

The photo insertion pipeline then corrects three visible inconsistencies. The EXIF orientation is respected in the editor. Clicking in the library no longer silently replaces an already filled block simply because it is selected. Finally, the aspect ratio used to insert a photo into a book is based on the actual cover area and not on an approximation.

The common thread is trust. A portrait-oriented image must not rotate when added to the project; a photo that has already been placed must not disappear with the next click; a cover must not be given dimensions calculated for an inside page.

Automatic filling becomes a product feature

The second half of the day sees the start of the most visible task: the automatic filling of notebooks. An initial version is able to distribute the photos, then subsequent iterations add improved layout, an action to clear the result and a confirmation prompt tailored to this destructive operation.

The photo album no longer merely offers blank pages. It begins to help the customer produce a complete book, whilst clearly retaining the option to undo changes.

What the day has in store

Twenty commits in six hours drive the product forward, but also increase the scope for regression. The following day will be devoted to drag-and-drop, spreads, layers and Fabric contracts. This is not a contradiction: 24 June saw the emergence of a genuine notebook function; on 25 June, we must learn how to make it stable.

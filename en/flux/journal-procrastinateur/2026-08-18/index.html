Day 130 – Save before you exit, choose before you migrate

Day 130 of the diary: the publisher is tightening up backup, checkout and responsiveness procedures, whilst an audit is calling into question the instinctive migration from R2 to Hetzner.

Session timetables

  • Tool used: Codex, with the Impeccable review and hardening workflow for the publisher
  • Primary environment: Mac, multiple KEREDIT worktrees, GitHub Actions and supplier search
  • Date covered: Tuesday 18 August 2026
  • Main branches: codex/impeccable-editor-hardening merged into dev via PR #443; storage audit retained uncommitted in codex/vrifier-ltat-de-la-branche
  • Time window used for the minimum: 17:44 → 19:00 CEST, from the creation of the storage analysis to the worktree snapshot
  • Minimum time observed: approximately 1h16+
  • Released version: 0.22.4, merge PR #443 f2b46361
  • Key milestones: storage analysis created at 17:44, seven hardening commits, green CI and journey, final Codex review with no major issues, snapshot a276f248
  • Minimum cumulative time since the start of the log: approximately 842h53+ (841h37+ as at 15 August, plus 1h16+ today)

Quick read

Two forms of validation are being worked on in the same window. In the editor, KEREDIT must be certain that a project has been saved before allowing the user to exit or submit. In the infrastructure, a desire to leave Cloudflare R2 must not result in a migration to the first European provider that comes along.

The first set of features is delivered in 0.22.4. The second remains a local analysis, not yet committed. Yet both apply the same rule: do not confuse a reassuring interface with a proven guarantee.

The backup becomes a barrier

The publisher now blocks both exit and command operations until a strict backup has been confirmed. Version conflicts remain distinct from transient errors: the former require a decision, whilst the latter must be able to be queued and retried.

The checkout explains more clearly why it is unavailable. The save statuses, keyboard controls and ARIA states are aligned. At medium width, the editor, the Template Builder and its client-side test no longer leave two side panels competing for space; the wide layout retains its behaviour.

PR #443 was short in duration but yielded a wealth of feedback. Several reviews were requested on successive HEADs to correct the CI security checks, responsive coverage, panel consistency and the requeueing of autosave. The CI and the ‘journey’ workflow are approved, and the final review finds no further major issues. The merge takes place at 6.47 pm.

Exiting R2 without triggering another outage

The storage audit begins with an encouraging finding: KEREDIT is already largely S3-compatible. Objects are referenced by key, URLs are signed on the fly and the backend hides the provider. The migration can therefore be a simple switchover of configuration and data.

However, the research rules out Hetzner as a viable option. The Object Storage service is experiencing a prolonged capacity degradation, with no SLA suited to a synchronous upload flow. OVH 1-AZ and Scaleway have also experienced recent incidents. The analysis identifies OVH Standard 3-AZ Paris as the leading candidate and IONOS Frankfurt as an alternative to test, but no migration decision has yet been implemented.

The real takeaway of the day is not a provider’s logo. It is the need for retries with backoff, proper tracking of 5xx errors and a copy with a second provider. Changing buckets without these safeguards would merely shift the vulnerability.

The pace is faltering

The log has already been interrupted for ten days. Yet the code continues to progress in dense batches, with repeated reviews and a new infrastructure decision to be made. The contrast is becoming difficult to bear: the technical evidence is piling up, but the sense of progress is no longer keeping pace. This day does not yet bear the most candid words on exhaustion; it reveals the conditions leading to it.

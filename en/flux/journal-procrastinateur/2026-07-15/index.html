Day 114 – Measure before storing

Day 114 of the log: the operational limits of the agents become clear, and photo storage is encrypted before deciding on whether to use ‘moderation’, ‘retention’ or ‘archiving’.

Quick read

After two days of migration and large-scale integration, 15 July brings no new client features. It transforms incidents and volumes into decision rules.

The first issue concerns agents: an asynchronous review must be clean on the current HEAD before merging, and an issue never constitutes implicit authorisation to create costly external infrastructure.

The second issue concerns photos: their storage cost is actually quite low, but they multiply rapidly at a technical level. The real key is not to make the client feel guilty or to choose the cheapest provider down to the last penny. It is to decide what is worth keeping, for how long, and in what reproducible form.

Incorporating the wait into the agent’s workflow

The ‘resolve’ skill now includes an explicit Codex review gate. After each push, the HEAD of the pull request is recorded, a new review is requested, and the merge remains blocked until the bot has responded correctly for that exact SHA.

A green CI, a review of an old commit or a few minutes’ wait do not constitute validation. Each finding is categorised; valid comments result in the smallest possible correction and a targeted test, after which the cycle restarts. If evidence is lacking, the agent halts.

This delay is not a slowdown. It prevents us from confusing automation with impatience, especially on days when several PRs follow one another within minutes.

Turning the R2 mirror into a sustainable boundary

The project memory records the previous day’s incident. In a test environment, a full 44.7 GB copy may be technically justifiable for a recovery plan, whilst remaining disproportionate to the immediate need.

The rule is now explicit: a backup audit remains read-only. It takes stock of the volume, explains any gaps, quantifies the cost and proposes a plan. The creation of a bucket, a large mirror, external storage or a recurring automation task requires prior approval from Marc.

The lesson extends beyond R2. Authorisation to resolve a problem must never be silently transformed into authorisation to expand the infrastructure.

A 4 MB photo becomes almost 23 MB

The GOF report cross-references the PRINTBOX 2025 activity with a sample of 100 Pixel photos processed through the KEREDIT pipeline. The average original file is 4.0 MB. KEREDIT adds approximately 0.013 MB for a thumbnail, 0.45 MB for a preview and, most significantly, 18.2 MB for a print-ready TIFF.

The total comes to 22.7 MB per photo, or 5.67 times the size of the original. The TIFF file accounts for 80.3 per cent of storage. This figure immediately shifts the debate: the production derivative, which can be regenerated from the original, takes up far more space than the browsing tools visible to the customer.

PRINTBOX recorded 2,389 folders in 2025, but not the number of source photos. The report therefore considers three scenarios: 15, 35 or 80 photos per folder. The base-case scenario represents approximately 83,600 photos per year.

With a PDF reserve of 220 GB and a 90-day photo retention period, the steady-state capacity reaches approximately 688 GB. At the Scaleway rate used in the study, this amounts to approximately 11 euros (excl. VAT) per month. Retaining all variants for one year brings the central scenario to 2.12 TB and approximately 34 euros (excl. VAT) per month.

Storage is therefore not free, but it is not the main financial risk. The lifespan of the print-ready TIFF file is the real multiplier.

Moderation must serve the client’s interests

A KEREDIT working paper sets out the following principle: retain for the long term the elements that make up the creative work, not everything that has been added around it.

The first proposed indicator is not an abstract quota in gigabytes. It aligns the import process with actual usage: 412 photos importées · 47 utilisées · 365 inutilisées. The product makes recommendations before imposing restrictions, reminds users that photos can be added later, and suggests a selection tailored to the number of pages or slots.

During creation, the filters ‘Utilisees’ and ‘Non utilisees’ are designed to help clean up the library. Upon request, KEREDIT can suggest either deleting photos not included in the document or retaining them for a further 30 days. Any expiry must be notified before the import, reiterated before deletion and logged.

The audit trail distinguishes between originals, thumbnails, previews, print-ready files and final PDFs. It rejects made-up ‘carbon’ equivalents, guilt-tripping and charging for every upload. Paid archiving only makes sense if it sells a comprehensible service: retrieval, reprinting, duration and guarantee.

This document remains a working proposal not yet integrated into dev as of the evening of the 15th. The proposed thresholds and durations do not yet constitute a client contract.

Measurements to be taken before September

The beta version must now record some simple metrics without storing the content of the photos: files selected, files sent, bytes per variant, photos used, photos unused at the time of order, and the time between import, production, reprinting and deletion.

After 50 to 100 representative orders, the median and P95 per product family will be able to replace the assumptions. The retention policy will then be able to be based on actual usage rather than on the assumption of infinite storage.

15 July is therefore not a meaningless pause. It is the moment when KEREDIT begins to distinguish between three things that are often conflated: what the technology allows, what the agent is authorised to do, and what the product promises the customer.

Day 131 – The release is drawing near, my body’s saying ‘enough’

Day 131 of the log: the POC and benchmark from the VPS have selected OVH 3-AZ Paris to bring customer data back into a European infrastructure.

Session timetable

  • Browser used: Codex
  • Primary environment: Mac, KEREDIT repository dépôt local krampouz, OVH and IONOS consoles, local S3 POC
  • Date covered: Thursday 20 August 2026; full report to follow after the session ends
  • Branches observed: codex/vrifier-ltat-de-la-branche then the branch from PR #444 merged into dev
  • Time window selected for the minimum: 16:08 → 19:12 CEST, from initial work on the POC to the final commit of the GOF audit
  • Minimum time observed: approximately 3 hours 4 minutes+, excluding research, supplier trials and conversations that cannot be reconstructed with a time stamp
  • Version delivered: 0.22.5, merge PR #444 d1a3acee
  • Main result: OVH Standard 3-AZ Paris validated via POC and benchmark from the VPS; IONOS abandoned; no migration carried out yet
  • Key logs: storage health admin page, R2/OVH benchmark, release 0.22.5 and audit of the GOF integration up to 3db0ed44
  • Minimum cumulative time since the start of the log: approximately 845h57+ (842h53+ as at 18 August, plus 3h04+ today)

Quick read

The storage POC provides a viable solution. OVH Standard 3-AZ Paris accepts the current KEREDIT stream: CORS, pre-signed PUTs with and without checksums, multipart, signed GETs and cleaning operations all pass. IONOS has been ruled out prior to technical testing due to issues with its onboarding process, a poor interface and an SLA that is too weak to make the effort economically viable.

Another infrastructure option, Forgejo, has been explicitly classified as zero priority. GitHub remains the source of truth and nothing should be migrated in advance. These two decisions narrow the technical scope. They do not automatically reduce the workload.

Sovereignty becomes a concrete architecture

The choice is not merely about a faster storage bucket. KEREDIT handles photos, projects and production documents belonging to OOBLIK’s clients. The aim is therefore to keep their data journey within Europe: the in Germany, object storage with OVH in France, in the Paris 3-AZ region. Moving away from R2, a service provided by an American company, removes the primary storage from the scope of the Cloud Act and makes the location more transparent.

This sovereignty is not presented as absolute. Cloudflare services may still be used outside the storage environment, and a European architecture does not exempt organisations from the need for backups, encryption or access control. The benefit of the day is more specific: client objects now have a French target that is proven to be compatible, whilst the application remains hosted in Germany.

The POC opts for OVH, with one caveat

The backend/scripts/poc-object-storage.mjs framework performs the functions on which KEREDIT actually depends. On OVH 3-AZ Paris, the bucket responds, the CORS rule is applied, the browser preflight is correct, simple and multipart uploads are successful, and signed GET URLs work. From the local workstation, the small GET request has a median time of around 32 ms and the 1 MB PUT takes around 570 ms. The measurement will need to be repeated from the VPS.

The SHA-256 checksum is accepted in the signature but is not validated by OVH: a deliberately corrupted body still receives a 200 response. This is not a blocking issue for the current workflow, which already places the checksum in the URL and confirms the upload on the backend side, but integrity then relies on TLS and this application-level validation. The log retains this caveat rather than turning a green POC into absolute certainty.

The benchmark is then run again from the production VPS. Compared to R2, OVH is approximately 2.6 times faster on the median for the 4 MB pre-signed PUT, 5.5 times faster for the small signed GET and 1.6 times faster for the 1 MB SDK PUT. Data sovereignty therefore does not require sacrificing performance in this case; the more variable p95 of the large PUT requests remains to be monitored.

The bucket and test keys must be revoked, as the credentials have been transmitted over an unsecured channel. No R2 copies, no switchover and no creation of production resources are initiated in this post.

Close the doors that do not lead to September

Forgejo is technically feasible, but it would affect repositories, issues, PRs, CI, GHCR, procedures gh and the Codex review. Now is not the right time. The ops documentation therefore sets out a decision not to take action: GitHub remains in place and any future evaluation will begin with an empty, isolated and reversible instance.

The storage migration itself is not yet a priority over the product. The useful outcome is a prepared plan and a roadmap: measure the actual volume, strengthen retry mechanisms and monitoring, and only then plan for mirroring, failover and rollback.

Rant: finally writing about burnout

The delay in posting this journal entry isn’t just a routine oversight. As the early September release approaches, the accumulated fatigue has become overwhelming: severe demotivation, doubts about my ability to meet the deadline and a feeling of being on the verge of burnout. I’m going through what feels like a mild depression, probably fuelled by exhaustion. Even tangible progress is no longer always enough to restore my drive.

I want to write about this honestly, because this state of mind is directly affecting the project: difficulty in wrapping up the day, temporarily giving up on the journal, decisions that feel more burdensome, and the impression that the release is drawing closer faster than the product is progressing. This post is not intended to dramatise the situation or to conclude that everything is going badly. It simply records a moment when continuing at the same pace is probably no longer sustainable.

I’m not going through this alone, though. I’ve had a rest, and Claire, my wife, is supporting me with her love. Her presence doesn’t take the fatigue away, but it changes the way I cope with it. In my own words today: ‘with love, everything passes’. This is also part of the truth of this period, and it deserves to be recorded in the journal just as much as the doubts.

The code repository tells a paradoxical story. Since the last post, the Builder has gone through the entire build chain, the print quantities have been made more reliable, the checkout process has been hardened and the storage option has been tested. So it is not the lack of results that is fuelling the doubt. It is the accumulation of issues, the approach of September and the gradual disappearance of any leeway to catch up.

The next milestone isn’t a new feature

The point is not to promise yet another sprint. We must first make the release clear: what is essential for early September, what is already sufficiently reliable, and what can wait. The log resumes today precisely for this reason. It is not just for counting commits; it must also prevent the project from overshadowing the person driving it.
